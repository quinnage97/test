# How to use this

You are a director of the company that runs your block, or you look after
blocks for other people. This does two jobs for you. Neither of them is
advice — everything it makes is built out of figures you type in.

---

## Job one: the service charge documents

### Set the block up once

Add the building. Two parts of that page matter more than the rest:

- **Who demands the service charge.** The company's full name, and an address
  in England or Wales where notices can be served on it. This has to appear on
  every demand. If it is missing, the charge can be treated as not being due —
  so the app will not let you finish a pack without it.
- **The flats, and each flat's share.** The share comes from the lease. They
  should add up to 100%. If they don't, the app will tell you, and it is worth
  finding out why before you send anything.

Then, once, go to **Settings** and paste in the summary of rights and
obligations. There is a link on that page to the exact wording. This has to go
out with every demand, and the wording is fixed by law, so the app will not
invent it for you and will keep reminding you until you have pasted it in.
Ten minutes, once, forever.

### Each year

Start an accounting year. Type in what the building spent, one line per thing:
insurance, cleaning, the lift, the roof repair. For each one, put in **the date
the cost was incurred** — not when you paid it. That date matters: a cost
incurred more than 18 months before you demand it can be unrecoverable, and the
app watches for it.

Add the reserve fund figures and the total you demanded on account during the
year. Then press **Produce the documents**.

### What you get

A demand for every flat, the summary of rights, the accounts, the annual report,
and the insurance information. You can read each one, copy it, or print it
(**Print or save as PDF** does both).

Before them, you get a short list of things to check. Two kinds:

- **Deal with this first** — something that could cost you. The app will not let
  you save the pack until it's sorted.
- **Worth a look** — read it and decide. You're the director.

### Then record what you sent

Open each document and fill in who it went to, how, and when. This is the boring
bit and it is the bit you will be glad of. If a leaseholder ever asks when they
were sent their accounts, or the tribunal does, the answer is in **Issued**,
and there is a button to download the lot as a spreadsheet.

Nothing in that record is ever changed or deleted. If you get an entry wrong,
just record it again — both stay, and that is the point.

## Job two: the heat network

**This only applies if your block has shared heating** — one boiler or plant
room heating the flats, rather than each flat having its own boiler.

If it does, your block is very likely a heat network, and heat networks are now
regulated by Ofgem. The part that should get your attention: operating one
without being registered, after the deadline, is expected to be a criminal
offence. Not a fine on the company. An offence.

Most blocks in this position have no idea. If yours has a shared boiler, work
through this.

### Three steps

1. **The scope test.** Six questions about how the heating works and who runs
   it. It gives you a likely answer and shows its reasoning. It says "likely"
   because that is honestly all it can say — the final answer depends on your
   leases and your contracts.

2. **The registration pack.** Everything a registration is expected to ask for,
   in one place: who would register, what the plant is, how many homes it heats,
   how residents are charged, who maintains it. Fill it in over a few evenings
   rather than on the day. The app lists what is still missing.

3. **The ongoing conditions.** Registering is the start, not the end. There are
   obligations that run for as long as the heating runs — telling residents what
   they're paying for, having a complaints procedure, knowing who would be at
   risk if the heat went off. Tick them off, and write down where the evidence is.

The number of days left is on the front page, along with where that date came
from.

## Two things to know about what this is

**It is not advice.** Every document says so, and that isn't the app being
modest. It doesn't know what your lease says. It can't tell you whether a cost
is reasonable or whether you're allowed to charge for something. It produces
documents from your figures and keeps the record. If something turns on the
lease, ask a solicitor or a surveyor.

**A lot of it isn't settled law yet.** The new service charge rules from the
2024 Act need further regulations before they take effect, and those haven't
been made. So the forms in here are the app's own working layouts — sensible,
readable, carrying the information they need to carry, but not the official
form, because there isn't one yet.

You'll see this everywhere:

> **Expected — not settled**

That means exactly what it says. Where a rule is settled, it says **In force**
instead. Every one of them shows you its source and the date somebody last
checked it. **What the law says** lists all of them on one page.

Producing these documents now is still worth doing. The information is what your
leaseholders are entitled to either way, and when the official forms appear the
figures are already in one place.

---

## If something looks wrong

Nothing you have typed is ever lost. Close the page and open it again.

If a document says it "still needs" something, it means a box is empty
somewhere — usually a leaseholder's name, or the company's address. Fill it in
and produce the pack again.
